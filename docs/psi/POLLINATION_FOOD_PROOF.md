# Pollination → Food — decision-grade candidate proof

**Status:** evidence-hardened candidate / not Gold / not founder accepted / not a public effectiveness ranking.

## Traversal

`Wild + managed pollinators → animal-mediated pollination → crop pollination → food production / dietary diversity / farmer livelihoods → pressures → Pollinator decline [4P-PX-0021] → relevant PSI interventions → evidence → implementation/place context → gaps → possible 4PLANET action`

Western honey bee (`Apis mellifera`, GBIF taxon 1341976) is one taxon example. It is **not** a proxy for all pollinators or for wild-pollinator diversity.

## Evidence claims

| ID | Direction | Claim | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| CLM-POLL-001 | SUPPORTS | FAO describes pollination as a direct link between wild ecosystems and agricultural production and reports that roughly three quarters of major food crop types depend at least partly on pollinators. | Crop-type dependence is not the same as share of calories, total tonnage or economic value. |
| CLM-POLL-002 | SUPPORTS | FAO reports that pollinators affect about 35% of global crop production by volume. | “Affected” is not equivalent to production that would disappear without pollination. |
| CLM-POLL-003 | SUPPORTS | Garibaldi et al. (Science, 2013) found positive associations between wild-insect visitation and fruit set across 41 crop systems; honey-bee and wild-insect visitation contributed independently. | Fruit set is not identical to farm profit, nutrition or population persistence. |
| CLM-POLL-004 | CHALLENGES | Managed honey bees should not be treated as a universal substitute for diverse wild pollinators. | Honey bees remain important crop pollinators; the challenged proposition is substitution. |
| CLM-POLL-005 | SUPPORTS | Dainese et al. (Science Advances, 2019) found biodiversity-mediated ecosystem-service benefits relevant to crop production across a global synthesis. | This does not imply that every biodiversity intervention raises yield everywhere. |
| CLM-POLL-006 | QUALIFIES | Zamorano et al. (2020) found field-margin floral enhancements increased pollinator abundance/richness at field edges but showed no consistent spillover into crop interiors or crop-yield effect. | The evidence base was Northern-Hemisphere heavy and relatively few studies assessed yield. |
| CLM-POLL-007 | QUALIFIES | A quantitative synthesis of flower strips/hedgerows found pollination effects variable and influenced by distance, age and floral diversity. | Average service effects must not be converted into guaranteed yield or economic outcomes. |
| CLM-POLL-008 | SUPPORTS | Artsdatabanken reports that in Norway’s 2021 Red List, 17% of assessed wild bee/bumblebee species were threatened and 30.6% were Red-Listed. | Norway-specific status, not a global trend estimate. |
| CLM-POLL-009 | SUPPORTS | Norway has a cross-sectoral action plan for wild pollinating insects for 2021–2028 focused on knowledge and more/better habitat. | Policy existence is not ecological outcome evidence. |
| CLM-POLL-010 | CHALLENGES | “Flower strips reliably increase crop yield” is not supportable as a universal statement from the reviewed synthesis evidence. | Positive results can occur; the rejected claim is universal/reliable effectiveness. |

## Major current gaps

1. **Implementation evidence:** mapped pollinator interventions lack normalized implementation-level outcome records in PSI.
2. **Economics:** no normalized cost/effect observations are attached to the mapped pollinator interventions.
3. **Geography:** global synthesis must not be silently transferred to every crop/species/place context.
4. **Negative evidence:** null and context-dependent results must remain visible.

## Decision-grade definition used here

Decision-grade means the vertical can support bounded, source-traceable comparison in the represented contexts without reconstructing the research from scratch. It does **not** mean universal effectiveness, certification, recommendation, transferability guarantee, a “best solution” conclusion or human expert validation.

## Dependency chain

Pollinator taxa / communities  
→ animal-mediated pollination  
→ crop pollination  
→ food production / dietary diversity / farm livelihoods  
→ pressures including habitat simplification and pesticide exposure  
→ Pollinator decline (`4P-PX-0021`)  
→ context-dependent intervention choices.

## Intervention 1 — Flower-rich margins / habitat mosaics

PSI ref: `4P-SOL-000086`  
Canonical type: INTERVENTION  
Evidence status: MODERATE / CONTEXT DEPENDENT

Supported:
- quantitative synthesis supports improved ecosystem-service delivery under some flower-strip designs;
- effects vary with distance, age/perenniality and floral diversity;
- habitat-diversification implementations can generate farm and biodiversity benefits in some contexts.

Qualified / challenged:
- pollination and crop-yield responses are variable;
- universal “flower strips reliably increase crop yield” language is rejected;
- habitat plantings alone do not guarantee reduced pesticide exposure.

Implementation/economic contexts:
- Morocco Farming with Alternative Pollinators: 233 farmer fields across four agro-ecosystems; study-reported biodiversity/pest and net-income outcomes are represented as **study outcomes**, not globally transferable effect estimates;
- England SFI26 CIPM2: GBP 798/ha/year public payment for flower-rich margins/blocks/strips; represented as a **financing/incentive observation**, not intervention cost or effect size.

## Intervention 2 — Threshold-based IPM

PSI ref: `4P-SOL-000068`  
Canonical type: INTERVENTION  
Evidence status: STRONG IN REPRESENTED FIELD SYSTEM

A four-year commercial-scale, replicated multi-site US corn/watermelon experiment found threshold-based IPM used 95% fewer insecticide treatments than conventional management. Corn yield was not reduced by omission of a neonicotinoid seed treatment; IPM watermelon had higher wild-pollinator visitation and 26% higher yield in that system.

Boundary:
- this does not establish that every IPM programme is pollinator-friendly;
- crop, pest, pesticide, climate, landscape and management context constrain transferability.

## Intervention 3 — Pesticide-risk reduction integrated with habitat planning

PSI ref: `4P-SOL-000087`  
Canonical type: INTERVENTION  
Evidence status: STRONG PRESSURE MECHANISM / MODERATE INTERVENTION CONTEXT

Supported:
- field and multi-field evidence support pesticide hazard/exposure as a material wild-bee pressure;
- habitat availability and pesticide-risk management should be evaluated together;
- exposure/effects vary among bee taxa.

Counterevidence / design warning:
- pollinator plantings adjacent to conventionally managed blueberry fields did not by themselves lower pesticide exposure/risk;
- managed honey bees or commercial bumble bees cannot be assumed to proxy all wild-bee exposure or response.

## Monitoring / measurement

The EU Pollinator Monitoring Scheme proposal is represented as measurement infrastructure, not ecological intervention proof. The proposed minimum scheme includes at least 2,120 monitoring sites and an estimated EU-wide cost around EUR 13.3 million/year over ten years.

## Decision questions now answerable

1. Should flower-rich habitat be described as universally yield-positive? **No.**
2. Can threshold-based IPM reduce insecticide use without sacrificing production in at least some real field systems? **Yes, with explicit context limits.**
3. Should habitat and pesticide-risk interventions be evaluated jointly? **Yes.**
4. Can the system name a universal best intervention? **No.**
5. What must be measured before transfer? Pollinator community, pesticide exposure, crop/pest context, habitat quality, yield/economic outcomes, persistence and maintenance.

## Remaining gaps

- no globally harmonised cross-intervention cost-effectiveness dataset;
- long-term pollinator population outcomes are thinner than short-term visitation/service/yield evidence;
- transferability across crops, landscapes, pesticide regimes and pollinator communities remains contextual;
- provider-specific OFFERING evidence is not populated and no offering is invented;
- independent human expert review of this assembled 4PLANET vertical has not occurred.

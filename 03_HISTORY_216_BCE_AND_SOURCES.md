# HISTORICAL ANCHOR — Cannae / Canusium, SECOND PUNIC WAR, 216 BCE
Research stance: start with source-supported historical situation; make alternative history possible through player decisions. Do NOT present invented or approximate details as facts.

## Anchor source: Polybius, Histories, Book III, chapters 107–118
https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/ancient/polybius-cannae.asp
https://penelope.uchicago.edu/thayer/e/roman/texts/polybius/3*.html
Polybius recounts Hannibal moving from Geronium to the Cannae citadel as harvest arrived. The Romans had stored grain and supplies around Canusium, collecting them at Cannae to supply the field force. Polybius describes Cannae as a damaged/ruined settlement, but the citadel and supplies as strategically significant. Roman alarm concerned lost provisions and command of the surrounding district. The confrontation culminated in the disastrous Roman defeat at Cannae in 216 BCE near the Aufidus (modern Ofanto). Hannibal's Iberian, Celtic, African and Numidian contingents, his cavalry advantage, and the Roman army's weight shaped the classic encirclement. Interpret ancient authors critically; specific numbers and precise formations vary in scholarly literature.

## Context essential to correct storytelling
The Second Punic War lasts 218–201 BCE; a long game can include Hannibal's advance from Iberia over the Alps, northern/central Italy, Lake Trasimene (217 BCE), Fabius' avoidance strategy, Cannae (216), post-Cannae Italian defections including Capua, Sicily and Spain, Roman reorganisation, Scipio's campaign in Africa and Zama (202 BCE). This is a proposed campaign chapter sequence, not content currently built.
At 216 BCE Rome is a Republic, NOT the later Roman Empire: no imperial legion uniforms, architecture or office titles should be imposed uncritically on this scenario.
Rome controls an alliance network: Latin and Italian allies have agency and loyalty; do not depict all southern Italian peoples as a homogeneous modern-style Roman nation. Tarentum's historical alignment in 216 BCE differs from the prototype's neutral tag; its later defection in 212 BCE is crucial. Capua's position evolves after Cannae. Syracuse's politics evolve later, notably after Hiero II. Faction dates require validation and dynamic allegiance.
A physical satellite map from 2016 is useful for coastlines, relief and major river valleys; its buildings, land use and road network are NOT evidence of BCE conditions.
